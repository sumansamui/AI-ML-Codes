Here is a 10-line report on black holes:

A black hole is a region in space where the gravitational pull is so strong that nothing, including light, can escape.
It is formed when a massive star collapses in on itself and its gravity becomes so strong that it warps the fabric of spacetime.
The point of no return around a black hole is called the event horizon, which marks the boundary beyond which anything that enters cannot escape.
Black holes are characterized by their mass, spin, and charge, which affect the strength of their gravitational pull.
The most massive black holes can have masses billions of times that of our sun.
They are found at the centers of galaxies, including our own Milky Way.
The study of black holes is an active area of research in astrophysics and cosmology.
Black holes are also believed to play a role in the formation and evolution of galaxies.
Their strong gravity can distort spacetime around them, creating strange visual effects such as gravitational lensing.
Despite their mysterious nature, scientists continue to learn more about black holes through observations and simulations.
